## The positioning: combinations, not counts

Competitors have styles. A style alone is one axis. This app gives three, and they multiply:

**870 user-chosen setups across both surfaces** — and the two surfaces use *different* dials,
which is the part that must be stated correctly:

| Surface | Dials | Combinations |
|---|---|---|
| Cinema | 12 styles × 5 daylight × 6 camera angles | **360** |
| Product Photography | 6 lighting × 5 angles × 8 environments | 240 |
| Cinematic Ads | 6 lighting × 5 angles × 8 ad environments | 240 |
| Fashion Photography | 6 lighting × 5 angles | 30 |
| | | **870** |

**Do not write "360 cinematic and marketing looks from 12 styles, 5 daylight conditions and 6
camera angles."** Those three dials are the cinema axes; they do not generate the marketing
looks, so that sentence credits marketing output to inputs that don't produce it. It is also
undersells the app by 510.

Note the lesson from TikTok while writing this: *counting features loses, stating an outcome
wins.* "870 setups" is an outcome. "12 styles, 5 lighting, 6 angles" is a list — and lists
scored 297–303 there while outcome framing scored 768.

**Small honesty note on Fashion:** its environment is picked at random in code
(`pick(FASHION_ENVIRONMENTS)`), not chosen by the user, which is why it contributes 30 and not
240. That also slightly undercuts "chosen, not guessed" for that one category — the lighting
and angle are chosen, the backdrop isn't. Harmless in a headline, worth knowing before anyone
claims full control across all three marketing categories.

### ⚠️ On the competitor comparison — do not put it in the listing

You framed this as "they have Tokyo Noir but not combined with angle and lighting." That's a
good *strategic* read and it should shape the copy. It must **not** become a written claim:

- `brand/claims.md` already bans one comparative claim — *"more presets than \<competitor\>"* —
  because it turned out to be false (Higgsfield ships 50–100+ camera-motion presets).
- I cannot verify what any competitor's app offers today, so any comparison I write would be
  unverified by construction, and this project's single most-corrected failure mode is exactly
  that.
- Apple's review guidelines also discourage referencing other apps in metadata.

**The combination claim is self-sufficient.** "Pick the style, the light and the angle" is
differentiating on its own merits without asserting anything about anyone else.

## The €5 Apple Search Ads test

**Set expectations honestly: €5 is a probe, not a campaign.** Typical cost-per-tap on niche
App Store terms runs roughly €0.30–1.50, so €5 buys somewhere between **3 and 16 taps**. At a
realistic install rate that is *one to five downloads*. It cannot move your weekly number.

What it *can* do — and this is why it's worth running — is tell you **which search term people
actually tap**, which is information you currently have none of.

**Use Advanced (not Basic).** Basic hands Apple your budget and gives you no keyword-level data,
which defeats the entire purpose here.

**Bid on long-tail exact matches, not head terms.** Avoid "ai video generator" and "ai art" —
high competition, and €5 disappears into a single tap or two.

| Keyword (exact match) | Why |
|---|---|
| `ai prompt generator for video` | The phrase the whole ASO change targets. Confirms the fix works. |
| `cinematic prompt generator` | Closest to what the app literally is. |
| `ai video prompt app` | Long-tail, likely cheap. |
| `prompt generator for kling` | Generator-specific; very low competition. |

Set a **low daily cap (€1–2)** so it runs several days rather than emptying in an hour — you
need a spread of impressions, not one burst.

**Read it as a keyword test, not a growth test.** After it exhausts, the useful output is the
tap-through rate per keyword. Whichever term earns taps is the term the metadata should lean
into further; whichever gets impressions but no taps is a positioning problem on the product
page, not a bid problem.

**Run it after 1.8.4 is live, not before.** Ads point at the product page, and the new subtitle
is doing the conversion work.
