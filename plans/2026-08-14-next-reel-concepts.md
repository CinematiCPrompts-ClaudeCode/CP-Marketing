## ★ #1 — RECOMMENDED: "Lazy prompt vs. real prompt" (before/after transformation)

**Why this one.** Before/after transformation is the single best-performing shape we have, and
it's been unused for ~3 months:

| Post | Platform | Views |
|---|---|---|
| "Amateur to Hollywood in 1 click" | TikTok | **1987** — best post ever, any platform |
| "This is your AI video without a cinematic prompt…" | TikTok | **845** |
| "This is what AI video looks like without…" | Instagram | **301** — IG's #2 ever |

It also sells the lead selling point (less guessing, fewer wasted credits) by *showing* it
instead of stating it — and August's post-mortem says TikTok pays for product-pain framing
(821–859) and punishes showcase framing (230–297). This is maximally product-pain.

**Concept.** One scene, two prompts, hard cut. Use the example straight out of
`brand/positioning.md`: *"a woman waiting on a street corner."*
- **Side A (before):** that plain one-line prompt, generated as-is. Flat light, dead centre
  framing, generic look.
- **Side B (after):** the same idea run through the app — **Tokyo Noir** style + **blue hour**
  lighting + **Dramatic Low** angle. Same subject, same moment, transformed.

Side B carries selling point #2 (style + lighting + angle, all three) while the cut itself
carries selling point #1. One reel proves both.

**Integrity note — this matters.** Side A must be a *genuinely naive* prompt someone would
really type, not a sabotaged strawman. If we hobble the "before" the comparison is a lie, and
it's the kind of thing that gets noticed. The real gap is big enough without help.

**Structure (target 12–15s, matching the 14.4s otter cut):**

| Time | Screen | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–0.3s | **Side B first** — the finished cinematic frame, full brightness | — |
| 0.3–2.0s | Hard cut back to Side A (the flat version) | `You typed this.` |
| 2.0–4.0s | Hold Side A, let it look boring | `Same idea. Same generator.` |
| 4.0–5.0s | **Whip cut to Side B** | `This is the prompt doing the work.` |
| 5.0–11.0s | Side B breathes — slow push in, show the light and the low angle | `Tokyo Noir · blue hour · Dramatic Low` |
| 11.0–14.0s | End card | App Store badge + `FEWER WASTED CREDITS` |

**Open on the payoff frame, not the flat one.** The otter cut's mistake was leading with its
darkest, slowest-reading frame. Here the brightest/most striking frame (Side B) flashes first
for ~0.3s as a cold open, *then* drops to the "before" — the viewer knows within one frame that
something good is coming, which is what buys the next 2 seconds.

**Hook lines, per platform** (final captions get written into a real package + gated later):
- **TikTok:** "You're not bad at AI video. Your prompt is." — pain-first, product-directed.
- **Instagram:** "This is what your prompt looks like without the light picked." — ultra-short,
  the 2-line shape that produced 717/301/272.
- **YouTube:** "Same AI Scene, Two Prompts — Lazy vs. Cinematic (Cinematic Prompts App)" —
  searchable feature title. Note the YT before/after title got only 16 views in June, so lead
  the title with the *scene comparison* framing (261 views, "Same AI Scene: Golden Hour vs…")
  rather than the word "Without".
- **Facebook:** hold. FB is at 6.8 avg views and under diagnosis — don't spend a new concept
  there until the Aug 19 reach test reads out.

## #2 — runner-up: "An envelope. A stairwell." narrative micro-story
Narrative still works, but **the hook line carries it, not the genre**. Compare July 3's
"An envelope. A stairwell. Nobody says a word." → **830**, against Aug 9's "Prenzlauer Berg
1986" → **290**. Same format, 3x gap: the first is an intriguing cold open, the second is just
a title. The Feb–Mar narrative peaks (The Crime 1868, The Conspiracy 1030, The Car Chase 930)
all had concrete-noun hooks too. Strong concept, but it sells the *vibe* rather than the
product — worth doing, worth doing *after* the transformation reel re-establishes the pitch.

## #3 — runner-up: v1.8.3 "6 shoot environments" feature announcement
Genuinely new (1.8.3 shipped this week: Seamless White Sweep, Neutral Studio, Lifestyle Scene,
Nature Setting, Futuristic, Luxury Interior) and feature announcements have a real ceiling —
"Now Pick Your Camera Angle" hit **1033** on YouTube, "New Cinematic Style: Nature" hit **1554**
on Facebook / 599 on YouTube. **But** it must be framed as *one* specific capability with a
pain attached, never as a list: "12 Cinematic Styles 5 Lighting Styles 6 Camera Angles" got
**297** on TikTok. Best used as a YouTube-led piece, since that's where feature titles index.
Holding it because the transformation concept beats it on TikTok, which is the volume channel.

# Credit planning — the full variant menu

**Unit = one generation** (one clip/still out of your generator). I don't know your per-credit
rate — it varies by generator, model and duration — so everything below is in generations;
multiply by your own rate.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** generation counts for *past* posts are my estimates inferred from
what's on screen, not logged production data. The view numbers are real; the cost column is a
reconstruction. Treat views-per-generation as a planning heuristic, not a measurement.

## The finding that should drive your budget

**The cheapest shapes are the highest-returning ones, and the most expensive shape is the
worst.** Ranked by TikTok views per generation:

| Shape | Gens | Proven TikTok views | **Views / gen** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before/after transformation** | ~2 | 1987, 845 | **423 – 994** |
| **Product / commerce (no-crew framing)** | ~2 | 859, 782 | **391 – 430** |
| Narrative micro-story | ~5 | 1868, 830 | 166 – 374 |
| Camera-angle demo | ~4 | 784 | ~196 |
| Multi-style variety showcase | ~12 | 768, 297, 482 | **25 – 64** |

The 12-style showcase costs **6× the credits of a transformation reel and returns a fraction
per credit.** "12 Cinematic Styles 5 Lighting Styles 6 Camera Angles" burned ~12 generations
for **297 views**.

**So: buy three cheap proven reels before one expensive showcase.** Six generations spent as
2+2+2 below gets you three posts each grounded above 780. Twelve generations spent on one
style-swap gets you one post grounded at 768.

## Variants, cheapest first

### 🟢 Tier 1 — 2 generations each (best return, start here)

**V1 · "Lazy prompt vs. real prompt"** — the #1 concept above. 2 gens.
Grounded: 1987 / 845 TikTok, 301 IG.

**V2 · "No studio. No photographer. No shoot." — product edition.** 2 gens.
Grounded: **859** TikTok (the exact triple-negation) and **782** (luxury Vespa ad). Shoot a
single product on the new v1.8.3 **Seamless White Sweep** environment, plus one "real world"
comparison frame. Negates costs a creator *actually pays* — which is precisely why the safari
version died at 230. Also the one concept where `#aiproductphotography` is legitimately in-scope.

**V3 · "Same scene, midnight vs. midday."** 2 gens.
The cheapest possible lighting demo — night and sunny only, no five-step cycle. Grounded: 821
("Stop regenerating just to fix your lighting"). Use this if you want the lighting pitch again
without paying for another 5-generation cycle.

### 🟡 Tier 2 — 3–5 generations

**V4 · Camera angle, three-pick.** 3 gens — Dramatic Low, Bird's Eye, Close-Up.
Grounded: **784** TikTok, **1033** YouTube (YT's #2 post ever). Don't shoot all six; three
angles carry the idea and cut the cost in half. **Strongest YouTube-led option on this list.**

**V5 · Five lights on a non-wildlife subject.** 5 gens.
Reuses the otter reel's template — a street corner, a person, a car. August showed
wildlife-as-subject drags on TikTok (230, 279) while the *lighting mechanism* sells fine (821).
Same production pipeline, better-fitting subject. Low marginal effort since the template exists.

**V6 · Narrative micro-story.** 4–5 gens.
Grounded: 830 ("An envelope. A stairwell.") — but note 290 for "Prenzlauer Berg 1986". The hook
line carries this, not the genre. Only worth the credits if the opening line is concrete and
intriguing.

### 🔴 Tier 3 — 6–12 generations (justify before spending)

**V7 · v1.8.3 six shoot environments.** 6 gens.
Real news value and a real ceiling on YouTube/FB (1033, 1554, 599) — but frame it as *one*
capability with a pain, never a list. As a list it's the 297 post. **YouTube-led only.**

**V8 · Twelve-style swap.** 12 gens.
Grounded 768 TikTok at best, 297 at worst, 25 IG, **0 FB**. Worst credit efficiency on the
board. Only buy this if you want one big evergreen "everything the app does" asset and are
willing to pay list-showcase prices for it.

## Recommended first batch — 6 generations

**V1 (2) + V2 (2) + V3 (2).** Three posts, three different proven hooks, every one grounded
above 780 on TikTok, total cost half of a single style-swap reel. Run them across three weeks
so each gets a clean read, then reinvest based on which one actually moved.

If you have room for 9: add **V4 (3)** as the YouTube-led piece, since YouTube is the one
platform where a feature title has hit 1033 and it's holding steadier than FB or IG.
